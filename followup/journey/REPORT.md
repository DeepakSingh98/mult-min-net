# WSP-JOURNEY: does gradient descent keep a destination it found itself, and is the loop to blame?

Run 2026-11-08, 08:34 to 10:50 UTC, on GCP: one n2-highcpu-32 Spot VM and one L4 Spot VM. Planning spend was
**$3.09 of the $100** the user set. Both VMs are deleted.
- Predictions: [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md), written before any run.
- Changes: [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md).
- Every number: [TABLES.md](TABLES.md), generated by `analyze.py` from the raw JSON in `results/`.
- Raw output stayed in cloud storage; the small JSON receipts are in `results/`.

## Why

The follow-up post says gradient descent walks away from a network that already works. Two open questions:
1. Does it also walk away from networks it found itself?
2. Is the any-length network's habit of breaking first on long problems caused by the shared loop?

The trained networks used:
- two Neural GPU seeds, trained on binary multiplication up to 20 bits;
- one looped transformer from the same family as the any-length net, trained on decimal addition up to 8 digits.

## Short version

1. **Training moves every working network, but it wrecks only the written ones.**
   - **Written networks.** Adam at every learning rate from 1e-6 to 1e-3 breaks them by the first check (step 25),
     at all seeds and both confidence levels. At that check they get **all 256 test problems wrong** (median).
   - **Trained networks.** At lr 1e-6 they mostly last 2,000 steps (15 of 18 runs). At 1e-3 they fail by step 25,
     like the written ones. At 1e-5 and 1e-4 they fail later (median step 40 to 1,000). They fail gently: the median
     failure is **1 to 3 problems wrong out of 256**, typically after the weights have moved 0.4% to 2% of their norm.
2. **The gradient points at fragile directions in every network, trained or written.** A step along the gradient
   breaks a network sooner than a random step does: 3× sooner (any-length), 500× (the 487-weight net), 10 to 230×
   (trained nets). I predicted trained nets would not show this (P1), and that was wrong. So this is not what makes
   written networks special.
3. **What does separate them: the gradient sees the harmless fix only in trained nets.** The harmless fix is scaling
   the readout, which raises confidence without changing any answer.
   - Trained nets: the gradient's cosine with that direction is 0.38 to 0.56.
   - Written nets: it's 3e-10 (any-length) and 7e-14 (487-weight).
4. **The loop explains why the long problems break first.** With the last 22 passes untied (each pass gets its own
   copy of the weights, starting equal), a step along the gradient has to be **37× larger** to break the network:
   9.8e-6 against 2.6e-7. The first failure moves from 100-digit problems to 4-digit problems in 8 of 8 draws.
   - Plain SGD at lr 1e-12 now lasts 2,000 steps at all 3 seeds, where the tied network dies by step 100.
   - At larger rates the untied net still dies, just later: step 1,300 vs 100 at 1e-11, and step 150 vs 25 at 1e-10.
     The last pass is still brittle on its own.

## Experiment 5: written vs trained networks

The table mostly shows the `low` condition: written nets as written, and trained nets with the readout scaled down to
the same loss (CE 0.52), so both are pushed equally hard. The `high` condition gives the same picture (TABLES.md).

| | any-length (written) | 487-weight (written) | Neural GPU s1 | Neural GPU s2 | addition transformer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| eps* along gradient / random | 0.33 | 0.0019 | 0.064 | 0.011 | 0.011 |
| cos(gradient, harmless fix) | 3e-10 | 7e-14 | 0.48 | 0.38 | 0.56 |
| Adam 1e-6, 2,000 steps: runs lasting (both conditions) | 0/6 | 0/6 | 6/6 | 3/6 | 6/6 |
| Adam 1e-5 / 1e-4 / 1e-3: runs lasting | 0/18 | 0/18 | 1/18 | 0/18 | 4/18 |
| median step of first failure, all Adam runs | 25 | 25 | 25 to 400 | 25 to 600 | 25 to 1,025 |
| problems wrong at the first failing check (median of 256) | 256 | 256 | 1 | 3 | 2 |

Notes:
- Each network's own recipe also fails the strict test (0/6 for each). That recipe is its training optimizer
  restarted from scratch, and a fresh Adam's first steps move every weight by about the learning rate. That is a jolt
  the original run never had, because its optimizer state was not saved. For example, the addition transformer at its
  own lr of 1e-3 fails by step 25.
- For seed 1 of the Neural GPU, length 64 was dropped from the strict test: it already gets one of the 32 problems
  wrong at the start. This is the protocol's rule, logged in DECISIONS.md.
- Trained nets also break first on their longest problems (20 bits, 64 bits, 8 digits).
- The cosine row is the `low` condition. As trained (`high`), the Neural GPUs give the same values and the addition
  transformer gives 0.049. The written nets give 8e-11 and 2e-15. The gap holds either way.
- "Problems wrong" counts each net's 256-problem set, over failures on that set, including the own-recipe runs. 12 of
  the second Neural GPU's failures are on its 64-bit set only (32 problems), so they're not counted. Split by learning
  rate: at 1e-5 and 1e-4 the trained nets' median is 1, and at 1e-3 it's about 25. `figures/make_followup_train.py`
  in the post folder recomputes this.

## Experiment 6: untie the loop (any-length network)

| | eps* along gradient | eps* random | first failing length (8 draws) | SGD lr 1e-12, runs lasting 2,000 steps |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| tied (as written) | 2.6e-7 | 7.9e-7 | 100 in 8/8 | 0/3 (die by step 100) |
| untied (last 22 passes) | 9.8e-6 | 9.9e-6 | 4 in 8/8 | 3/3 |

At the start, the untied network's gradient sits entirely on the last pass's copy: the earlier copies hold 1.5e-15 of
its squared norm. Untied and tied logits are identical at the start (checked locally, max |difference| 0.0).

## Predictions

| | prediction | result |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | trained nets: gradient no more fragile than random (R >= 1) | **wrong**: R = 0.004 to 0.10 |
| P2 | cos(gradient, harmless fix) >= 0.1 trained, <= 1e-6 written | **held** |
| P3 | trained nets last 2,000 steps at Adam <= 1e-4 and with their own recipe; written fail everywhere | **wrong for trained** (they last only at 1e-6); written failed everywhere, as predicted |
| P4 | untied eps* >= 5× tied, and the first failure is not at 100 | **held** (37×, 8/8) |
| P5 | untied SGD lasts at some lr where tied dies | **held** (1e-12) |

- **Experiment 5 by the frozen rule: "no difference".** Trained nets also have R <= 0.33, and they also fail the
  strict test at Adam 1e-5 and 1e-4.
- **My post hoc reading:** the direction is the same but the size is not. Training nudges trained nets off a problem or
  two after hundreds of steps. It knocks written nets over completely in fewer than 25 steps, at every learning rate.
- **Experiment 6 by the frozen rule: the length effect comes from the shared loop.**

## What this does not show

- Only three trained networks, of two architectures, on tasks next to the written ones (binary multiplication,
  decimal addition), not the same tasks.
- Strict means every one of 256 problems is right, which is a harsh bar for trained nets. Their "failures" are
  mostly one or two problems.
- Fresh Adam restarts. No optimizer state was available.
- Nothing about training from random.
